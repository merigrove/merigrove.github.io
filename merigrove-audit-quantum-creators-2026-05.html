Overview

Where Quantum Creators sits today

Quantum Creators is a fast-scaling online education and coaching business generating approximately €120,000 in monthly revenue, with a cost base of roughly €70,000 and a unit economics profile that is materially better than the founders realise. The business is founder-dependent, intuition-driven, and operating without the financial architecture needed to support the next phase of growth.

Gallo's audit responses reveal a founder who is commercially sharp, financially literate in a transactional sense, and acutely aware that the current model is not sustainable as the business scales. The risks are not existential today. They become existential at 2x or 3x the current size if the infrastructure is not built now.

This report identifies nine priority pain points drawn directly from the audit responses, then provides a structured 90-day playbook to resolve them. The scope covers financial reporting and controls, unit economics and data infrastructure, the sales-to-finance data layer, and the operational systems needed to begin extracting Gallo and Miguel from day-to-day execution.

Part One

Identified Pain Points

Nine priority issues surfaced from the audit questionnaire. Each is grounded in a direct response from Gallo and assessed for severity and urgency.

01
Critical
No formal P&L, cash reporting, or structured monthly close

Gallo manages finances through a manual, transaction-by-transaction cash-basis process. There is no structured P&L, no cash movement report, and no formal management accounts produced on a monthly cycle. Decisions are made intuitively based on cash in the account rather than on a clear picture of profitability. Gallo himself acknowledges he cannot state gross margin or net margin without looking it up.

"I track everything on a cash flow basis. Transactions... I do a lot of decisions almost intuitively if I see the ROI and the treasury allows it."

02
Critical
No unit economics visibility: margin per deal, LTV, LTV:CAC

The business has a single core offer but no formal calculation of contribution margin, cost per acquisition by channel, or LTV:CAC ratio. CAC is not tracked as a standing metric. Without this data, pricing decisions, channel allocation, and sales compensation cannot be optimised. The business is flying without instruments at €1.4M ARR run-rate.

"LTV:CAC I don't actually know but LTV is getting higher and higher." "I don't know my contribution margin at current price points."

03
Critical
No financial forecasting or budget against which to measure

The business operates without a formal financial plan, annual budget, or monthly variance analysis. The only stated goal was to exceed €1M post-tax in year one. Without a budget, there is no mechanism to detect deterioration early, assess whether ad spend is productive in context, or hold team leads accountable to financial targets. The recent €5k cash surplus month is a direct symptom of this.

"No. We have roughly goals personally and professionally. We set budgets and goals monthly." "Last month we made only 5k€ cash surplus. We made bad decisions in marketing."

04
High Priority
Cash management is reactive, not proactive

There is no cash flow forecast, no defined minimum reserve policy, and no structured approach to managing seasonal pinch points such as Spanish holidays or the December slowdown. The business holds approximately 1.5 months of operating costs in liquid cash, below the three-month minimum for a business of this risk profile. Ad spend of up to €50,000 per month creates substantial volatility against a €110,000 reserve.

"No. I don't. We have been 'surviving' for the first year and reinvesting everything." "We didn't manage those seasons properly."

05
High Priority
No structured sales-to-finance data bridge

Sales data sits in Close CRM and a Looker Studio dashboard, but this data is not formally translated into financial metrics. Average revenue per deal, closer earnings per deal, setter conversion rates and their financial impact, cost per booked call, and cost per closed deal are not tracked as standing metrics. The sales team cannot see the financial consequence of their conversion rate, and Gallo cannot see the financial cost of each sales motion at a granular level.

"We have a Looker Studio Dashboard that connects sales and marketing. We only advertise on Meta." Close CRM tracks opportunities but not financial unit-level outcomes per rep.

06
High Priority
Collections are informal and founder-dependent

90% of payments are taken by manual bank transfer with no structured follow-up sequence for late payments. Gallo uses personal incentives such as 1-on-1 access to collect outstanding balances. This is not scalable, creates aged debt that is managed informally, and ties founder time to a finance administration function. As deal volume grows, this will create increasingly significant cash flow drag.

"It is handled informally. We take 90% of payments via manual bank transfers. I do a lot of bonuses like 'I give you 1on1 with me if you liquidate the payment'. It takes my time as a founder."

07
High Priority
Unresolved statutory and tax compliance exposure

Statutory filings including VAT returns, corporation tax, and payroll obligations are described as being in a "grey zone" with a Cyprus restructure being planned but not yet executed. There is no external accountant in an advisory capacity, only transactional management of accounts. This represents a material compliance and financial risk as the business approaches the scale where authorities scrutinise more closely.

"No. We are on a grey zone. Planning to move to Cyprus the company." "No external accountant."

08
High Priority
Founder dependency creates a single point of failure across all functions

If Gallo is unavailable: deal volume drops by more than 50%, the sales team loses its manager, salaries and banking cannot be processed, accounting stops, and fires across departments go unmanaged. If Miguel is unavailable: all new content stops, both paid and organic, and a significant portion of business coaching delivery is lost. The business is structurally designed around two people who cannot be absent. This is a ceiling on growth, not a strength.

"If Gallo is out (that is impossible to happen). First thing: closed deals drops 50% or more, management of the sales team, paying salaries and managing bank accounts, accounting, sales coaching on the delivery side."

09
Medium Priority
Incomplete SOPs and undocumented institutional knowledge

Between 30% and 40% of operational processes have documented SOPs, and those that exist are described as not fully up to date. The client onboarding process involves multiple manual steps owned by individual closers or the founders. Without documented processes, the business cannot hire effectively, onboard staff to standard, or identify and eliminate waste systematically. Automation opportunities in delivery and sales follow-up are identified but not yet acted upon.

"I would say that we have SOPs for between 30% and 40% of tasks in the business. Some are not up to date." "Yes, probably a ton [of automation opportunities], especially on the delivery side and sales."

The pattern behind the pain points

Every pain point above connects to a single root cause: the business was built for execution speed, not for scalability. Gallo and Miguel made excellent decisions to get to €120k/month. The same decisions that created that velocity are now the ceiling. The next phase requires a different operating model, not faster running of the existing one.

Critically, this business has strong underlying economics. The ad spend is large but it is generating real revenue. The cost base is lean. The offer is working. The opportunity loss from not having the data infrastructure and financial architecture in place is significant. This is a high-return intervention, not a remediation exercise.

Month 1
Foundation Build what should already exist
Finance
Monthly Reporting Architecture
  • Consider migrating to a structured accounting platform. Tools such as QuickBooks or Xero are worth exploring for the longer term, but this is not a prerequisite to getting started. A well-structured Google Sheets setup with a defined chart of accounts and consistent categorisation will work effectively for now, and is the right starting point before investing in software overhead. What matters is discipline and structure, not the tool.
  • Define the monthly close cycle. Establish a fixed monthly close calendar: books closed and reconciled by working day 5, management accounts produced by working day 10. Define who owns each step. Initially this is Gallo with Merigrove oversight; the goal is to transition to a bookkeeper by month three.
  • Rebuild last month's financial statements as a standing template. Produce a clean P&L and cash movement report for the most recent completed month. These two documents become the monthly template that Gallo completes at every close. The P&L captures revenue, cost of sales, gross profit, operating expenses by category, and net profit. The cash movement report tracks opening balance, cash in by source, cash out by category, and closing balance. These are the two reports the business will produce every month, consistently, in the same format.
  • Define the three-report monthly pack. The standard reporting pack going forward is: the P&L, the cash movement report, and the unit economics summary. Nothing more is needed at this stage. Standardise the layout and tab structure now so the format is fixed and the close process becomes repeatable from month one.

Unit Economics
Gross Margin and Contribution Model
  • Build the contribution margin model per deal. Map every cost that is incurred as a direct consequence of closing a deal: closer commission (8% of cash), setter contribution (4% of cash plus base allocation), payment processing, onboarding cost, and delivery cost per client (coach time, Carlos time, platform seat). Calculate contribution margin in euros and as a percentage at the current €7,000 price point.
  • Segment by deal type. Distinguish between full-pay and payment plan deals. Payment plans carry additional collection cost and cash flow delay. The contribution margin on a payment plan deal is not the same as a full-pay deal and must be tracked separately.
  • Calculate CAC by channel. Ad spend is Meta-only, which simplifies this. Divide total monthly Meta spend by number of new clients acquired. Cross-reference against the Looker Studio data to get cost per booked call and close rate from call to client. This gives a true CAC and cost per pipeline stage.

Month 2
Instrumentation Activate the data layer
Data Infrastructure
Sales-to-Finance Bridge
  • Define the sales metrics that flow into finance. The following must become standing weekly metrics: deals closed (volume and value), average deal value, payment plan ratio, booked calls from ads, show rate, close rate, cost per booked call, cost per close, setter-level conversion rates, closer-level earnings generated per deal net of commission. These are not just sales metrics. They are financial metrics.
  • Create rep-level financial scorecards. Each closer and setter should be able to see, per week: how many deals they contributed to, the revenue generated, their commission earned, and the average contribution margin of their deals. This is not surveillance. It is the information needed to have financially grounded performance conversations.
  • Establish the weekly data cadence. Define a weekly rhythm: on Monday, the previous week's sales data is pulled and cross-referenced against ad spend and cash received. Gallo reviews a one-page summary. This replaces the current informal daily monitoring with a structured information ritual.

Finance
Monthly Cash Forecast
  • Build the monthly cash forecast template. A forward-looking monthly cash model maps every known cash outflow (ad spend, payroll, commissions, software) against expected cash inflows from confirmed deals and payment plan receipts. Built in the same Google Sheet as the reporting pack, it projects the next month's closing cash position based on current pipeline and committed costs. Updated at each monthly close, this is the instrument that prevents a repeat of the €5k surplus month. It provides the early warning the business currently lacks.
  • Set a minimum cash reserve policy. Formalise the rule: the business maintains a minimum of three months of operating costs, approximately €210,000, in liquid cash at all times. Ad spend budgets are adjusted before this floor is breached, not after.
  • Implement a monthly forecast review. On the same day management accounts are produced, review the prior month's forecast versus actual. Identify where the variance occurred. Refine the forecast inputs accordingly. Over time, the accuracy of the model improves and it becomes a genuinely reliable decision-making tool rather than a theoretical exercise.

Revenue Operations
Payment Collection Infrastructure
  • Migrate to card-on-file or automated payment collection. The reliance on manual bank transfers for 90% of revenue is the root cause of the collections problem. Implementing card-on-file via Stripe or an equivalent payment processor changes the model fundamentally: all new deals, full-pay and payment plan, are taken by card at the point of close. Payment plan instalments are charged automatically on the agreed schedule. This removes the collections function from Gallo's workload entirely and eliminates the personal-incentive tactics currently used to recover late payments.
  • Build the automated follow-up sequence for failed payments. Even with card-on-file, payments will occasionally fail due to card limits or insufficient funds. An automated sequence handles this without founder involvement: immediate notification to the client, retry on day three, automated follow-up on day five, closer escalation on day seven. This keeps the process consistent and measurable without manual intervention.
  • Track payment collection as a financial metric. Monitor the percentage of monthly revenue received within the expected collection window. Any deterioration in this metric is an early indicator of client quality issues, comp plan misalignment, or sales process gaps. It belongs in the monthly reporting pack.

Month 3
Systematisation Remove founders from execution
Operations
SOP Completion and Documentation Sprint
  • Audit the existing 30-40% of SOPs. Identify which documented processes are current and which are outdated. Retire outdated versions. For each active process, validate against how the work is actually being done today, not how it was done six months ago.
  • Prioritise and complete the critical 60%. Focus the documentation sprint on the processes that currently depend on Gallo or Miguel. Sales team management, sales coaching, accounting and payroll, client onboarding, and content briefing and publishing. Each SOP must be written to a standard where someone new could follow it without asking a founder for clarification.
  • Document the financial processes specifically. Monthly close procedure, budget review process, cash forecast update protocol, aged debt review, commission calculation and payroll processing. These are the finance function SOPs that remove Gallo from the accounting role and allow a bookkeeper to be onboarded.
  • Create a process ownership map. For every core process, assign an owner who is not Gallo or Miguel. Where no capable owner currently exists, identify whether the gap is a hire, a training requirement, or an automation opportunity.

People + Operations
Founder Extraction: Finance and Admin
  • Hire or contract a bookkeeper. By the end of month three, Gallo should not be doing the bookkeeping. A part-time bookkeeper (20 hours per month) working from the documented SOPs and the accounting system can own the close cycle, reconciliations, and commission calculations. Gallo reviews and approves. He does not produce.
  • Define Gallo's non-negotiable founder accountabilities. There are things only Gallo can do: closing large deals, setting sales strategy, making capital allocation decisions, and holding the team's standards. Codify this list. Every task not on the list is a delegation or automation target.
  • Define Miguel's non-negotiable founder accountabilities. Content strategy and the product vision are Miguel's domain. Client coaching for his key clients is Miguel's domain. Everything else is a build-out target: a content operator who manages the execution layer beneath Miguel's creative direction.
  • Activate identified automation opportunities. Using the N8N and AI capability already in the business, implement automations for: sales call confirmation and reminder sequences, payment plan follow-up, Skool onboarding triggers, and internal reporting data pulls. These remove manual touchpoints without removing quality.

Finance
Pricing and Margin Review
  • Conduct a formal pricing review using the contribution model. The last price increase (from €5k to €7k in September) was based on external advice rather than internal economics analysis. By month three, the contribution margin model will show what the minimum viable price is, what the contribution is at €7k, and whether there is headroom to increase. This decision should be data-driven.
  • Review the commission structure against unit economics. Closer commission at 8% of cash and setter contribution at 4% are significant cost lines. Model the impact on contribution margin of different commission structures, including tiered structures that reward higher close rates or higher deal values. The goal is not to cut commissions. It is to ensure alignment between comp design and the financial outcomes the business needs.
  • Establish the ongoing monthly finance review cadence. By end of month three, the following should happen every month without Merigrove's prompting: management accounts produced, budget versus actual reviewed, cash forecast updated, unit economics dashboard refreshed, sales-finance bridge report distributed to Gallo. This is the operating rhythm the business runs on for the next phase of growth.

Merigrove Assessment

The opportunity

Quantum Creators is one of the more financially promising businesses at this stage that Merigrove has audited. The underlying economics are solid, the offer is clearly working, and both founders have the drive and commercial intelligence to execute what is being prescribed here.

The gap is not talent or market fit. The gap is infrastructure. Gallo is operating as CFO, head of sales, head of finance administration, collections manager, and occasional firefighter simultaneously. Miguel is in a comparable position on the content and product side. Neither of them should be in those operational seats 90 days from now.

The 90-day programme above is designed to be done in parallel with the business running at full pace. Nothing here requires the business to slow down. It requires the founders to allocate deliberate time each week to building the system beneath them, rather than continuing to be the system.

At the end of 90 days, Quantum Creators should have: a clean financial reporting function it did not have to build alone, a data layer that translates sales activity into financial outcomes in real time, a documented operating model, and two founders who are spending the majority of their time on the work that only they can do. That is the version of this business that scales past €3M and beyond.
