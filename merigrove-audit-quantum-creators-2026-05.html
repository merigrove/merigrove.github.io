Finance & Ops Overview

Where Quantum Creators sits today

Quantum Creators is a fast-scaling online education and coaching business generating approximately €120,000 in monthly revenue, with a cost base of roughly €70,000 and a unit economics profile that is materially better than the founders realise. The business is founder-dependent, intuition-driven, and operating without the financial architecture needed to support the next phase of growth.

Gallo's audit responses reveal a founder who is commercially sharp, financially literate in a transactional sense, and acutely aware that the current model is not sustainable as the business scales. The risks are not existential today. They become existential at 2x or 3x the current size if the infrastructure is not built now.

This section identifies nine priority pain points drawn directly from the audit responses, then provides a structured 90-day playbook to resolve them. The scope covers financial reporting and controls, unit economics and data infrastructure, the sales-to-finance data layer, and the operational systems needed to begin extracting Gallo and Miguel from day-to-day execution.

Part One

Identified Pain Points

Nine priority issues surfaced from the audit questionnaire. Each is grounded in a direct response from Gallo and assessed for severity and urgency.

01
Critical
No formal P&L, cash reporting, or structured monthly close

Gallo manages finances through a manual, transaction-by-transaction cash-basis process. There is no structured P&L, no cash movement report, and no formal management accounts produced on a monthly cycle. Decisions are made intuitively based on cash in the account rather than on a clear picture of profitability. Gallo himself acknowledges he cannot state gross margin or net margin without looking it up.

"I track everything on a cash flow basis. Transactions... I do a lot of decisions almost intuitively if I see the ROI and the treasury allows it."

02
Critical
No unit economics visibility: margin per deal, LTV, LTV:CAC

The business has a single core offer but no formal calculation of contribution margin, cost per acquisition by channel, or LTV:CAC ratio. CAC is not tracked as a standing metric. Without this data, pricing decisions, channel allocation, and sales compensation cannot be optimised. The business is flying without instruments at €1.4M ARR run-rate.

"LTV:CAC I don't actually know but LTV is getting higher and higher." "I don't know my contribution margin at current price points."

03
Critical
No financial forecasting or budget against which to measure

The business operates without a formal financial plan, annual budget, or monthly variance analysis. The only stated goal was to exceed €1M post-tax in year one. Without a budget, there is no mechanism to detect deterioration early, assess whether ad spend is productive in context, or hold team leads accountable to financial targets. The recent €5k cash surplus month is a direct symptom of this.

"No. We have roughly goals personally and professionally. We set budgets and goals monthly." "Last month we made only 5k€ cash surplus. We made bad decisions in marketing."

04
High Priority
Cash management is reactive, not proactive

There is no cash flow forecast, no defined minimum reserve policy, and no structured approach to managing seasonal pinch points such as Spanish holidays or the December slowdown. The business holds approximately 1.5 months of operating costs in liquid cash, below the three-month minimum for a business of this risk profile. Ad spend of up to €50,000 per month creates substantial volatility against a €110,000 reserve.

"No. I don't. We have been 'surviving' for the first year and reinvesting everything." "We didn't manage those seasons properly."

05
High Priority
No structured sales-to-finance data bridge

Sales data sits in Close CRM and a Looker Studio dashboard, but this data is not formally translated into financial metrics. Average revenue per deal, closer earnings per deal, setter conversion rates and their financial impact, cost per booked call, and cost per closed deal are not tracked as standing metrics. The sales team cannot see the financial consequence of their conversion rate, and Gallo cannot see the financial cost of each sales motion at a granular level.

"We have a Looker Studio Dashboard that connects sales and marketing. We only advertise on Meta." Close CRM tracks opportunities but not financial unit-level outcomes per rep.

06
High Priority
Collections are informal and founder-dependent

90% of payments are taken by manual bank transfer with no structured follow-up sequence for late payments. Gallo uses personal incentives to collect outstanding balances. This is not scalable, creates aged debt managed informally, and ties founder time to a finance administration function. As deal volume grows, this will create increasingly significant cash flow drag.

"It is handled informally. We take 90% of payments via manual bank transfers. I do a lot of bonuses like 'I give you 1on1 with me if you liquidate the payment'. It takes my time as a founder."

07
High Priority
Unresolved statutory and tax compliance exposure

Statutory filings including VAT returns, corporation tax, and payroll obligations are described as being in a "grey zone" with a Cyprus restructure planned but not yet executed. There is no external accountant in an advisory capacity. This represents a material compliance and financial risk as the business approaches the scale where authorities scrutinise more closely.

"No. We are on a grey zone. Planning to move to Cyprus the company." "No external accountant."

08
High Priority
Founder dependency creates a single point of failure across all functions

If Gallo is unavailable: deal volume drops by more than 50%, the sales team loses its manager, salaries and banking cannot be processed, accounting stops, and fires across departments go unmanaged. If Miguel is unavailable: all new content stops, both paid and organic, and a significant portion of business coaching delivery is lost. The business is structurally designed around two people who cannot be absent.

"If Gallo is out (that is impossible to happen). First thing: closed deals drops 50% or more, management of the sales team, paying salaries and managing bank accounts, accounting, sales coaching on the delivery side."

09
Medium Priority
Incomplete SOPs and undocumented institutional knowledge

Between 30% and 40% of operational processes have documented SOPs, and those that exist are described as not fully up to date. The client onboarding process involves multiple manual steps owned by individual closers or the founders. Without documented processes, the business cannot hire effectively, onboard staff to standard, or identify and eliminate waste systematically.

"I would say that we have SOPs for between 30% and 40% of tasks in the business. Some are not up to date." "Yes, probably a ton [of automation opportunities], especially on the delivery side and sales."

The pattern behind the pain points

Every pain point above connects to a single root cause: the business was built for execution speed, not for scalability. Gallo and Miguel made excellent decisions to get to €120k per month. The same decisions that created that velocity are now the ceiling. The next phase requires a different operating model, not faster running of the existing one.

Critically, this business has strong underlying economics. The ad spend is large but it is generating real revenue. The cost base is lean. The offer is working. The opportunity loss from not having the data infrastructure and financial architecture in place is significant. This is a high-return intervention, not a remediation exercise.

Model the impact on contribution margin of different commission structures, including tiered structures that reward higher close rates or higher deal values. The goal is alignment between comp design and the financial outcomes the business needs.
  • Establish the ongoing monthly finance review cadence. By end of month three, the following should happen every month: management accounts produced, cash forecast updated, unit economics dashboard refreshed, and the sales-finance bridge report distributed to Gallo. This is the operating rhythm the business runs on for the next phase of growth.

The opportunity

Quantum Creators is one of the more financially promising businesses at this stage that Merigrove has audited. The underlying economics are solid, the offer is clearly working, and both founders have the drive and commercial intelligence to execute what is being prescribed here.

The gap is not talent or market fit. The gap is infrastructure. Gallo is operating as CFO, head of sales, head of finance administration, collections manager, and occasional firefighter simultaneously. Miguel is in a comparable position on the content and product side. Neither of them should be in those operational seats 90 days from now.

The 90-day programme above is designed to be done in parallel with the business running at full pace. Nothing here requires the business to slow down. It requires the founders to allocate deliberate time each week to building the system beneath them, rather than continuing to be the system.

At the end of 90 days, Quantum Creators should have: a clean financial reporting function, a data layer that translates sales activity into financial outcomes in real time, a documented operating model, and two founders spending the majority of their time on the work that only they can do. That is the version of this business that scales past €3M and beyond.
