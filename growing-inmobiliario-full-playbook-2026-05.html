Sales Diagnostic

Six critical issues. All of them fixable.

The audit identified six structural gaps in the sales operation. Taken together they mean the business is running at a significant discount on its revenue potential. The 30/60/90 roadmap below addresses each one in the correct sequence — stop the bleeding first, then systematise, then build predictability.

Critical — Immediate revenue risk
High — Material performance gap
Medium — Systems and scale gap
Critical

Single lead source — existential risk

100% dependent on Facebook Ads, already causing near-zero appointments this month. No cold outreach, no email system, no referral engine, no organic channel. One platform ban stops the business entirely.

Most urgent fix in the entire audit. This is not a sales problem — it is a business continuity problem.
Critical

Show rate at 50% — entirely fixable

The manager setter is at 85.5%. The other two are at 64.9% and 60%. This gap is not a lead quality problem — it is a pre-call process problem. No confirmation sequence, no warming sequence, no pre-call assets in place.

Setter show rate range: 60% to 85.5%. A 20-point spread on the same lead source is a process failure, not a talent failure.
High

25% close rate — needs systematic diagnosis

No documented post-call follow-up sequence. No call review cadence for closers. No data on deal velocity. Without knowing where deals break down, it cannot be fixed systematically.

Close rate diagnosis requires tagged lost deal data. Currently that data does not exist — which means the problem cannot be solved with confidence.
High

10.3% refund rate — qualification or expectation problem

6 refunds out of 58 clients in 90 days. Either the wrong people are being closed or promises on the call are not matching delivery. Root cause analysis is required before more closers are added.

Do not scale closing capacity until this is understood. Adding volume to a leaking bucket costs more than it earns.
High

No follow-up infrastructure at any stage

No pre-call sequence, no post-call sequence, no SOP for follow-up efforts. Miquel is responsible for follow-up but has no process, no SOP, and no tracking. Unknown how many follow-up attempts are made or how long leads are worked before going inactive.

Revenue is leaking at every stage of the funnel. No-shows are not being systematically re-engaged. Non-closed leads are not being followed up by design.
Medium

CRM health and team capacity risk

Deal stages are not being moved consistently. Tasks are not being set. Zero forecasting capability. Additionally, Pol is leaving the sales team on May 12, one setter is on a performance plan likely to be terminated, and two cold call setters are in ramp-up and not yet producing.

The team is thin and the CRM is unreliable. Both must be addressed before scale is attempted.

Phase One — Days 1–30
Stop the Bleeding
Focus: Revenue protection
Primary Objective

Protect revenue, fix the show rate, diversify lead sources, and stabilise team capacity. No scaling until existential risk from single lead source is addressed and show rate is trending upward.

Lead Diversification

Break the Facebook dependency

  • Cold calling setters: set KPIs and daily dial targets immediately; track booked call rate from day one
  • Email system: define ICP list, launch first sequence, track open rate, reply rate, and booked calls
  • Referral engine: build a simple referral ask into every onboarding and client touchpoint
  • Goal: by day 30, Facebook is no longer the only active channel producing appointments
Pre-Call Sequence

Build and launch immediately

  • Confirmation message on booking via WhatsApp and email
  • Reminder 24 hours before the call
  • Reminder 1 hour before the call
  • Short value-add or video to warm intent before they show up
  • This alone should move show rate from 50% toward 65%+ within 30 days
Refund Root Cause

Diagnose before scaling

  • Pull all 6 refund cases and identify whether the issue is qualification, expectation setting, or delivery
  • If qualification: tighten the lead form and setter qualification script
  • If expectation: review what is being promised on calls versus what is delivered
  • Do not hire or scale closing capacity until this is understood
Team Capacity & CRM

Stabilise the operation

  • Execute the setter exit cleanly and immediately; do not let the performance plan drag
  • Accelerate ramp-up on the two cold call setters: daily role plays, call reviews, KPI check-ins
  • Reduce pipeline stages to only what is actually being used
  • Mandate that every contact has a next-action task set before EOD; assign Miquel CRM health ownership
Day 30 Outcomes:
Cold outreach producing bookings Pre-call sequence live Refund root cause identified CRM stages cleaned
2
Phase Two — Days 31–60
Build the Systems
Focus: Process installation
Primary Objective

Install the processes that make performance repeatable and measurable. By end of day 60, the business should have a documented post-call follow-up SOP, a weekly call review cadence, tagged lost deal data, and calling infrastructure inside GHL.

Post-Call Follow-Up SOP

Build for every call outcome

  • No-show: immediate re-book attempt followed by 3-touchpoint sequence over 7 days
  • Not closed on the day: 5-touch follow-up sequence over 14 days
  • Inactive leads: define the threshold (e.g. 30 days no response = moved to nurture)
  • Miquel owns execution; Miquel gets a documented SOP and a tracking dashboard in GHL
Close Rate Improvement

Weekly call review cadence

  • Each closer submits one self-reviewed call per week
  • David reviews and gives written feedback within 48 hours
  • Monthly group call review session
  • Tag every lost deal with a reason: objection not handled, no urgency, partner not involved, financial disqualification
  • After 30 days of tagging, the data shows exactly where deals are being lost
GHL Infrastructure

Get the calling tool live inside GHL

  • WhatsApp is untrackable at scale; all outbound calls from setters must be logged, recorded, and reviewable
  • This is also critical for follow-up tracking and SOP enforcement
  • Define who has access, who reviews recordings, and on what cadence
Compensation & David Transition

Align incentives and management structure

  • Add a specific commission tier for upsell conversions; tie it to upsell closed, not just upsell offered
  • Define clearly what David's management responsibilities are week to week
  • Set a target for how much of his time is sales versus management
  • His 3% override on other closers must be tied to their performance improving
Day 60 Outcomes:
Post-call follow-up SOP live Weekly call review running Lost deal tags in place Calling inside GHL

Finance & Operations Diagnostic

Seven issues. Two are structural risks.

The audit identified seven distinct gaps in the finance and operations function. Two are classified as critical, meaning they represent active structural risk that should be addressed before any significant scaling. Three are high severity, requiring resolution within the first two months. Two are medium severity and form the later-stage layer of the playbook.

Critical — Active risk; address immediately
High — Material gap; resolve within 60 days
Medium — Important foundation; address in months 3–5
Critical

No formal management accounts or financial reporting

The business operates from a Google Sheet tracking automated expenses and income. There is no structured P&L, no monthly close process, and financial decisions are not driven by revenue data. The founders acknowledge they have never had anyone teach them to run a proper finance function.

"We use a Google Sheets where expenses and income are automated. We don't use revenue to make decisions."
Critical

Personal and business finances fully intermingled

Business and personal expenses are not separated. The founding CEO is paying team housing costs from personal funds. Gross margin calculations are distorted by this blurring, making it impossible to know the true cost structure of the business.

"Business and personal finances are NOT separated — Pol pays team housing costs from personal."
High

No cash flow forecast — zero liquidity visibility

There is no rolling cash flow forecast. The business has experienced months with zero in accounts. Cash shortfalls have been caused by over-investing in tools, programmes, and headcount without a systematic view of available cash.

"We need advice on this to do it correctly and systematically."
High

Collections process is entirely informal

Approximately €15k in client debts are outstanding. The post-close process involves manual steps across multiple people and systems with no formalised SOP and no accounts receivable ageing process.

"~€15k in outstanding debts. Some automations but no SOP."
High

Unit economics not embedded in decisions

Unit economics work has been done but is not actively used. Payroll runs at approximately 28% of revenue with no defined target or monitoring. Pricing decisions are not grounded in cost data. This produces the risk of scaling unprofitable revenue.

"Did unit economics work but don't actively use it. Payroll ~28% of revenue, no target %."
Medium

Organisational design lacks clarity and documentation

No org chart exists. Roles and responsibilities are informal. Key decisions sit with the founders by default, not by design. The business would halt within hours if either founder became unavailable. Responsibility for fulfilment is concentrated and fragile.

Key-person risk is currently rated critical. The business cannot scale if it cannot operate without its founders for 72 hours.
Medium

Process documentation is under 20% complete

Fewer than 20% of critical processes are documented. The onboarding SOP is partially built. No setter qualification SOP, no CRM hygiene SOP, no call review SOP exists. Knowledge sits with individuals, not with the business.

SOP coverage below 20% means the business runs on tribal knowledge. Every departing team member takes institutional knowledge with them.

Month One — Weeks 1–4
Financial Foundations & Forecasting
Addresses: 2 Critical, 1 High
Primary Objective

Establish clean financial foundations and immediate cash visibility. Separate personal and business finances, build the chart of accounts, stand up a rolling cash flow forecast, and map the organisational baseline. These four workstreams unlock everything that follows.

Finance Separation

Separate personal and business finances

  • Open dedicated business bank accounts if not already in place
  • Identify and reclassify all personal expenses currently running through the business
  • Define a clear rule for what qualifies as a business expense going forward
  • Reconcile outstanding personal transactions to establish a clean opening position
Chart of Accounts

Build a consistent financial categorisation system

  • Define all revenue streams: core programme, upsell tier, events
  • Define all cost categories: ads, software, payroll, external services, events
  • Backfill categorisation for the most recent three to six months
  • Document the categorisation rules so any future hire can apply them consistently
Cash Flow Forecast

Build a rolling 13-week cash flow model

  • Map all inflow timing: when clients pay, instalment schedules, payment split structures
  • Map all outflow timing: payroll, ad spend, software, external services, planned events
  • Build a weekly cash position forecast with a 13-week forward horizon
  • Define a minimum cash reserve policy (recommended: two months of fixed costs, approximately €80k)
Org Baseline

Map the current organisational structure

  • Document every current role and the person filling it
  • Map informal responsibilities that sit outside formal job titles
  • Identify where decisions currently land and whether that is by design or default
  • Flag the top five areas where the business halts if founders are unavailable
Month 1 Outcomes:
Personal and business finances separated Clean chart of accounts 13-week cash forecast live Org baseline documented
2
Month Two — Weeks 5–8
Reporting Infrastructure & Collections
Addresses: 2 High Issues
Primary Objective

Build the financial reporting layer and formalise how revenue is collected. By end of month 2, the business should have its first monthly P&L, a functioning AR process, and unit economics embedded in decisions rather than sitting as a dormant spreadsheet.

Management Accounts

Build a monthly P&L template and close process

  • Design a P&L structure by revenue stream and cost category, using the Month 1 chart of accounts
  • Produce the first monthly P&L for the most recent full month
  • Establish a monthly close target: within five business days of month end
  • Define who owns the P&L, who reviews it, and what decisions flow from it
Collections & AR

Formalise revenue recovery

  • Build a collections SOP: who chases, when, by what method, and what escalation looks like
  • Implement an AR ageing report in Holded or equivalent; review weekly
  • Recover the existing €15k in outstanding debts using the new SOP
  • Target: AR outstanding under €5,000 and average AR age under 30 days by month end
Unit Economics

Embed economics into decisions

  • Calculate contribution margin per programme type using the clean cost data from month 1
  • Set a payroll target range: 25–30% of revenue; review monthly
  • Define the cost-per-acquisition across all active lead channels
  • Embed unit economics review into the monthly P&L session
Gross Margin Validation

Establish the true margin baseline

  • Produce the first clean gross margin figure once personal and business finances are separated
  • Validate whether the current ~48% figure reflects the true cost structure or is distorted by mixed expenses
  • Identify whether Charlie's programme costs and external service costs should be included or excluded
  • Set gross margin as a core constraint in all future pricing and capacity decisions
Month 2 Outcomes:
First monthly P&L produced AR process formalised €15k debt recovery in progress Unit economics in monthly review

Month Three — Weeks 9–12
Organisational Accountability
Addresses: 2 Medium Issues
Primary Objective

Build the organisational clarity that allows the business to function without the founders in every routine operation. Define roles, set accountability structures, and reduce key-person dependency from critical to medium risk within 90 days.

Org Design

Define structure and ownership formally

  • Build a formal org chart showing all roles, reporting lines, and decision rights
  • Write job descriptions for every role that exists in the business
  • Define a RACI for the ten most critical operational processes
  • Identify which decisions currently require founder involvement and which should not
Key-Person Risk

Reduce to medium risk within this month

  • Identify the three processes that halt if either founder is unavailable for 24 hours
  • For each: document the process, train a deputy, and test the handover
  • Define the business continuity target: operational for 72 hours without founder involvement
  • Review succession readiness for each critical role
Accountability System

Install regular cadences and ownership

  • Define a weekly leadership meeting structure: who attends, agenda, outputs
  • Assign a named owner to every recurring operational responsibility
  • Build a simple scorecard: each function leader reports 3–5 metrics weekly
  • Set the first quarterly review: business-wide performance versus targets
Hiring Framework

Define the conditions for growth

  • Document the financial thresholds that must be met before any new hire is approved
  • Define the roles the business needs in the next 12 months and in what order
  • Build a simple onboarding checklist so new hires are productive in weeks, not months
  • Review current team capacity against the €1.5M revenue target
Month 3 Outcomes:
Formal org chart complete Key-person risk reduced to medium Weekly accountability cadence live Hiring framework defined

Month Four — Weeks 13–16
SOP Library & Process Documentation
Addresses: 1 Medium Issue
Primary Objective

Bring SOP coverage from under 20% to over 80% of critical processes. Documentation enables delegation, reduces key-person risk, and allows the business to train new hires without the founders being in the room.

Priority SOPs

Document the highest-risk processes first

  • Client onboarding SOP (partially built — finalise and validate)
  • Collections and AR SOP
  • Payroll processing SOP
  • Fulfilment delivery SOP for each programme type
Sales SOPs

Formalise the sales operation in writing

  • Setter qualification and call SOP
  • Pre-call sequence SOP
  • Post-call follow-up SOP
  • CRM hygiene SOP
  • Call review and coaching SOP
SOP Format Standard

Build a consistent documentation framework

  • Define the standard SOP format: owner, trigger, steps, outputs, review cadence
  • Store all SOPs in a single shared location accessible to the whole team
  • Assign a review date to every SOP so they do not become stale
  • Test each SOP with the person responsible for executing it before marking it final
Training Integration

Embed SOPs into onboarding

  • Every new hire reads and signs off on relevant SOPs in their first week
  • SOPs are the primary training tool, not ad hoc instruction from founders
  • Build a simple audit: random spot-check of SOP adherence quarterly
Month 4 Outcomes:
SOP coverage above 80% Shared SOP library live All SOPs tested and signed off Embedded in onboarding

Month Five — Weeks 17–20
Predictability & Scale
Completes the foundation
Primary Objective

Install the KPI and decision-making layer that makes business performance visible and predictable. By the end of month five, the business should have a live dashboard, a monthly review cadence, defined financial decision thresholds, and a 12-month scale plan backed by real unit economics.

KPI Dashboard

Build the business visibility layer

  • Define the 10–15 metrics that matter most: sales, financial, operational
  • Build a single dashboard that consolidates all of them in one view
  • Automate where possible; manual update where necessary with clear ownership
  • Founders review the dashboard weekly; leadership team reviews monthly
Monthly Review Cadence

Institutionalise the review rhythm

  • Monthly P&L review within five business days of month end
  • Comparison of actuals against forecast: variance analysis and root cause
  • Sales performance review: pipeline, show rate, close rate, refunds
  • Cash flow forecast update: 13-week rolling refresh
Decision Thresholds

Create financial decision guardrails

  • Define spend authorities: who can approve expenditure at each level (€1k, €5k, €10k and above)
  • Define the conditions under which a new hire can be approved: revenue level, margin, runway
  • Define the minimum cash reserve that must be maintained before scaling ad spend
  • Build a simple investment evaluation template for programmes and tools
Scale Planning

Plan the next 12 months with the foundations in place

  • Rebuild the revenue target model (€1.5M) backed by unit economics
  • Map the organisational structure required to deliver at that revenue level versus current state
  • Produce a 12-month hiring and investment plan with financial guardrails
  • Set the conditions for activating paid ad scale, currently bottlenecked by fulfilment capacity
Month 5 Outcomes:
KPI dashboard operational Monthly review cadence live Financial decision thresholds defined 12-month scale plan produced
