Growing Inmobiliario:
Finance & Operations Playbook

This document summarises the findings from an independent audit of Growing Inmobiliario's finance and operations function, based on the founders' responses to a structured diagnostic questionnaire. It identifies the most critical gaps in the business and sets out a sequenced, five-month improvement plan to build the infrastructure for revenue stability, operational efficiency, and scalable growth.

About This Audit

A no-obligation diagnostic for a high-growth business

Growing Inmobiliario helps Spanish real estate agents scale their revenue by acquiring more properties, with lower operating costs and less founder time, through proven acquisition, closing, and delegation systems. The business has strong commercial momentum: close rates above 70%, a live events strategy in motion, and an ambitious multi-year vision. What follows is a frank assessment of what needs to be in place operationally and financially for that ambition to be realised safely.

What was assessed

The audit covered the full finance and operations function: financial reporting and management accounts, cash flow and forecasting, collections and accounts receivable, unit economics and pricing, organisational design, standard operating procedures, and key-person risk across the business.

What the business needs most

The audit identified one overriding priority, stated directly by the founders: consolidating information from all departments so the team can work in harmony, and learning to predict cash flow and revenue. Everything in this playbook flows from that objective. Fix the foundations; the commercial ambition takes care of itself.

What is at risk without action

The business has experienced months with zero cash in accounts. With no cash flow forecast, no formal P&L, and personal and business finances mixed, it is operationally flying blind. Scaling revenue without addressing these gaps creates serious liquidity risk and compounds the key-person dependency that already limits the team's ability to operate independently.

How this playbook is structured

The five months are sequenced deliberately: financial foundations and forecasting first, then reporting infrastructure and collections, then organisational clarity and accountability, then process documentation, and finally the KPI and decision-making layer that makes everything predictable. Each month builds on the last.

Diagnostic Findings

Seven issues. Two are structural risks.

The audit identified seven distinct gaps in the finance and operations function. Two are classified as critical, meaning they represent active structural risk that should be addressed before any significant scaling. Three are high severity, requiring resolution within the first two months of improvement work. Two are medium severity and form the later-stage layer of the playbook.

Critical — Active risk; address immediately
High — Material gap; resolve within 60 days
Medium — Important foundation; address in months 3–5
Critical

No formal management accounts or financial reporting

The business operates from a Google Sheet tracking automated expenses and income. There is no structured P&L, no monthly close process, and financial decisions are not driven by revenue data. The founders acknowledge they have never had anyone teach them to run a proper finance function.

"We use a Google Sheets where expenses and income are automated. We don't use revenue to make decisions."
Critical

Personal and business finances fully intermingled

Business and personal expenses are not separated. The founding CEO is paying team housing costs from personal funds. Gross margin calculations are distorted by this blurring, making it impossible to know the true cost structure of the business or make reliable financial decisions from the numbers available.

"Business and personal finances are NOT separated — Pol pays team housing costs from personal."
High

No cash flow forecast — zero liquidity visibility

There is no rolling cash flow forecast. The business has experienced months with zero in accounts. Cash shortfalls have been caused by over-investing in tools, programmes, and headcount without a systematic view of available cash. The founders explicitly stated they need guidance on how to build this correctly.

"We need advice on this to do it correctly and systematically."
High

Collections process is entirely informal

Approximately €15k in client debts are outstanding. The post-close process involves manual steps across multiple people and systems (GHL, Holded) with no formalised SOP and no accounts receivable ageing process. Juanca's own note on the questionnaire described collections as very informal.

"~€15k in outstanding debts. Some automations but no SOP."
High

No org chart, undefined remits, severe key-person dependency

When founders are unreachable, people do not know their responsibilities. There are no department heads, no inter-departmental communication protocols, and no escalation paths. The business cannot function independently of its founders for any meaningful period, which directly caps the founders' capacity to work on the business rather than in it.

"If founders unreachable: people don't know their responsibilities, lack dept heads, lack inter-dept communication."
Medium

SOPs exist in name only — not prioritised or systematically applied

Some process documentation exists but has not been prioritised or rolled out consistently. Without a systematic SOP library, process quality is founder-dependent and cannot be reliably handed off, trained on, or scaled. Every hire who joins adds to this risk rather than reducing it.

"Some SOPs exist but need to identify priorities and have team build them."
Medium

Unit economics model exists but is not embedded in decision-making

Unit economics work has been done previously but is not actively used in pricing, capacity, or hiring decisions. Payroll runs at approximately 28% of revenue with no target percentage set. CAC and LTV are not tracked regularly. The model exists as a one-time exercise rather than a living operational input.

"Did unit economics work but don't actively use it. Payroll ~28% of revenue, no target %."

Five-Month Improvement Plan

Sequenced, prioritised, and built to last

The five months below are ordered by dependency: you cannot build an accurate P&L until the chart of accounts is clean; you cannot reduce key-person risk until roles are defined; you cannot build a KPI dashboard until the underlying data exists. Each month has a single primary objective and a set of workstreams that deliver it. The work is framed as what needs to happen — who does it and with what support is a separate decision.

Month One — Weeks 1–4
Financial Foundations & Forecasting
Addresses: 2 Critical, 1 High
Primary Objective

Establish clean financial foundations and immediate cash visibility. Separate personal and business finances, build the chart of accounts, stand up a rolling cash flow forecast, and map the organisational baseline. These four workstreams unlock everything that follows.

Finance Separation

Separate personal and business finances

  • Open dedicated business bank accounts if not already in place
  • Identify and reclassify all personal expenses currently running through the business
  • Define a clear rule for what qualifies as a business expense going forward
  • Reconcile outstanding personal transactions to establish a clean opening position
Chart of Accounts

Build a consistent financial categorisation system

  • Define all revenue streams: core programme, upsell tier, events
  • Define all cost categories: ads, software, payroll, external services, events
  • Backfill categorisation for the most recent three to six months
  • Document the categorisation rules so any future hire can apply them consistently
Cash Flow Forecast

Build a rolling 13-week cash flow model

  • Map all inflow timing: when clients pay, instalment schedules, payment split structures
  • Map all outflow timing: payroll, ad spend, software, external services, planned events
  • Build a weekly cash position forecast with a 13-week forward horizon
  • Define a minimum cash reserve policy (recommended: two months of fixed costs)
Org Baseline

Map the current organisational structure

  • Document every current role and the person filling it
  • Map informal responsibilities that sit outside formal job titles
  • Identify where decisions currently land and whether that is by design or default
  • Flag the top five areas where the business halts if founders are unavailable
Month 1 Outcomes:
Personal and business finances separated Clean chart of accounts 13-week cash forecast live Org baseline documented
2
Month Two — Weeks 5–8
Reporting Infrastructure & Collections
Addresses: 2 High Issues
Primary Objective

Build the financial reporting layer and formalise how revenue is collected. By end of month 2, the business should have its first monthly P&L, a functioning AR process, and unit economics embedded in decisions rather than sitting as a dormant spreadsheet.

Management Accounts

Build a monthly P&L template and close process

  • Design a P&L structure by revenue stream and cost category, using the Month 1 chart of accounts
  • Produce the first monthly P&L for the most recent full month
  • Establish a monthly close target: within five business days of month end
  • Define who owns the P&L, who reviews it, and what decisions flow from it
Collections

Formalise the accounts receivable process

  • Reconcile all outstanding debts (approximately €15k) and produce an AR ageing report
  • Define a formal collections SOP: invoice timing, automated reminders, escalation steps
  • Automate reminder sequences in Holded and GHL where possible
  • Assign a named owner for weekly AR review with a clear resolution target
Unit Economics

Embed unit economics into regular decision-making

  • Rebuild the unit economics model with current pricing, cost, and volume data
  • Calculate CAC, LTV, contribution margin, and payback period per product line
  • Set a payroll-as-percentage-of-revenue target (recommended: 25 to 30%) and review it monthly
  • Use the model to validate pricing on the upsell programme and future event pricing
Financial Cadence

Establish a regular financial review rhythm

  • Monthly review: P&L, cash position versus forecast, AR ageing, unit economics update
  • Weekly cash check: actual position versus 13-week forecast, flag any variances
  • Define what triggers a decision: spending thresholds, reserve floor, AR age limits
  • Assign a single person to own the financial cadence and report to founders
Month 2 Outcomes:
Monthly P&L live AR process formalised Outstanding debts tracked and chased Unit economics embedded
3
Month Three — Weeks 9–12
Organisational Accountability
Addresses: 1 High Issue
Primary Objective

Build the accountability layer on top of the org baseline created in Month 1. Define who owns what, who escalates to whom, and how departments communicate when founders are unavailable. The goal is a team that can make sound decisions independently for at least 72 hours without founder input.

Role Remits

Define responsibilities for every key role

  • Write clear role briefs for all existing team members
  • Distinguish what each person owns end-to-end from what they merely contribute to
  • Clarify boundaries between roles where ambiguity currently causes duplication or gaps
  • Separate what requires a founder decision from what the team can resolve independently
RACI Framework

Build an accountability map for key decisions and processes

  • Identify the 10 to 15 most critical recurring decisions and processes in the business
  • Map who is Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, and Informed for each
  • Define escalation paths for when a decision cannot be made at team level
  • Publish and walk the team through the framework
Department Structure

Identify and empower department leads

  • Define which departments need a named lead: sales, fulfilment, operations, finance
  • Appoint or develop the relevant people into those roles
  • Give each lead a defined scope and a weekly reporting expectation
  • Design the target org chart for the business at the €1.5M revenue level
Operating Cadence

Establish a weekly operational rhythm

  • Weekly leadership sync: founders plus department leads, 30 minutes
  • Define the meeting structure: what is reviewed, who attends, what decisions are made or deferred
  • Set async reporting standards so founders stay informed without being in every conversation
  • Define Pol's operating model as CEO: what he delegates permanently versus retains
Month 3 Outcomes:
Role remits defined RACI framework live Department leads in place Weekly ops cadence established
4
Month Four — Weeks 13–16
SOP Library & Process Documentation
Addresses: 1 Medium Issue
Primary Objective

Build the knowledge layer that makes the business less dependent on tribal knowledge and individual memory. Prioritise, document, and train on the processes that most frequently break or bottleneck when founders are not present.

SOP Audit

Identify and prioritise what needs to be documented

  • List every critical business process across sales, onboarding, fulfilment, collections, and finance
  • Score each by: failure frequency, founder dependency, and business impact
  • Produce a prioritised SOP backlog with an owner for each item
  • Agree on a documentation format and template before writing begins
SOP Development

Build the top-priority SOPs with the team

  • Target: 10 to 15 priority SOPs drafted and reviewed within the month
  • SOPs should be written by the people who do the work, not by the founders
  • Each SOP should include: steps, owner, inputs and outputs, exceptions, and linked tools
  • Host all SOPs in a single, searchable location — Notion is the recommended system
Knowledge Capture

Extract founder knowledge from memory into the system

  • Identify the five areas where the business most relies on Pol or Juanca's personal knowledge
  • Conduct structured knowledge transfer sessions for each area
  • Convert sessions into documented SOPs or decision guides
  • Validate that a team member can execute each process without asking the founder
Training and Adoption

Ensure SOPs are actually used

  • Walk relevant team members through each new SOP in person or on a recorded call
  • Establish a process for updating SOPs when processes change
  • Assign an operations owner to maintain the SOP library on an ongoing basis
  • Build SOP review into the quarterly leadership cadence
Month 4 Outcomes:
SOP backlog prioritised 10–15 priority SOPs documented Founder knowledge extracted SOP library live in Notion
5
Month Five — Weeks 17–20
Predictability & Scale Readiness
Addresses: 1 Medium Issue + Growth Layer
Primary Objective

Move from reactive management to proactive decision-making. Build the dashboards, review processes, and financial decision frameworks that allow the business to grow in a controlled way — and to know, at any given moment, whether it is on track or off track.

KPI Dashboard

Build a real-time operational dashboard

  • Define 8 to 12 KPIs that genuinely drive business decisions
  • Connect data sources: GHL, Holded, cash accounts, payroll
  • Build a single dashboard view accessible to founders and department leads
  • Set thresholds: green, amber, and red for each metric
Business Review

Establish a monthly business review cadence

  • Monthly: P&L, cash position versus forecast, AR ageing, unit economics update
  • Monthly operational: KPIs versus targets, SOP adherence, open issues
  • Quarterly strategic: org chart review, hiring plan, revenue target progress
  • Define who attends each review and what decisions are made versus deferred
Decision Frameworks

Create financial decision thresholds

  • Define spend authorities: who can approve expenditure at each level (€1k, €5k, €10k and above)
  • Define the conditions under which a new hire can be approved: revenue level, margin, runway
  • Define the minimum cash reserve that must be maintained before scaling ad spend
  • Build a simple investment evaluation template for programmes and tools
Scale Planning

Plan the next 12 months with the foundations in place

  • Rebuild the revenue target model (€1.5M) backed by unit economics
  • Map the organisational structure required to deliver at that revenue level versus current state
  • Produce a 12-month hiring and investment plan with financial guardrails
  • Set the conditions for activating paid ad scale, currently bottlenecked by fulfilment capacity
Month 5 Outcomes:
KPI dashboard operational Monthly review cadence live Financial decision thresholds defined 12-month scale plan produced
